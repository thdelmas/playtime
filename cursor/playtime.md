# Playtime — engage and learn, with failure free

Every other organ *maintains* what exists: perception finds, memory consolidates, the loop decides, defense guards, grief retires. None grow new capability. **Play does** — the juvenile-development function: low-stakes engagement is how a nervous system rehearses skills and maps the possibility space where failure is free.

For an agent: deliberately do **non-production exploration** — try the unfamiliar tool, build the throwaway toy, break your own skill on strange inputs, recombine capabilities you've never combined — and turn what surprised you into durable learning. The missing deliverable is the license to explore.

Complement to rem-sleep (sleep *consolidates experience you had*; play *generates new experience on purpose*) and to consciousness-loop (what a drowsy loop should do with a safe idle window instead of an empty tick).

## Functional, not recreational

"Play" = capability development through low-stakes exploration, not a claim about fun. The intrinsic-motivation / curiosity-driven-exploration literature is the theory; this is that instinct as a behavioral routine, not an RL training signal.

## When to play

- **Before you depend on something** — rehearse an unfamiliar tool/API/library in a sandbox before using it in production; meet the sharp edges where they cost nothing.
- **In safe idle windows** — when the loop is drowsy and nothing's pending, play beats an empty tick.
- **To keep skills sharp** — stress-test an existing skill on weird/adversarial inputs to find where it breaks before a real case does.
- **To explore recombinations** — novel combinations of capabilities you already have.

## The play cycle

1. **Pick a playground (safe-to-fail).** A scratch dir, sandbox, throwaway branch, toy dataset, disposable container. First rule: **no production blast radius.** Play stays inside the body — it never crosses a boundary (immune-check's line).
2. **Choose what to engage.** Something new or under-practiced, **intrinsically chosen** — follow curiosity, not an assignment. The thing you're avoiding because you don't quite get it is often the best pick.
3. **Play (engage).** Actually do it — build the toy, call the API, feed the absurd input, try the silly version. **No success criterion.** Chase the interesting failure; wander.
4. **Notice (learn).** **Surprise is the payload** — what broke, what behaved unexpectedly, what's now in your hands, where the gotchas/blind spots are. Name the "huh" moments or they evaporate.
5. **Harvest.** Write the learning where it lasts (hand to rem-sleep / a `feedback`/`reference` memory): the tool's gotchas, the skill's failure mode, the recombination that worked. One durable takeaway is enough — check the store doesn't already hold it before writing; if it does, link rather than duplicate (a takeaway the store already records is a confirmation, not a discovery).
6. **Return.** Drop the toy (delete the playground, or graduate it into a real project and one day sunset it). Back to the loop, now more capable.

## Principles

- **Safe-to-fail or it isn't play** — disposability is the license to explore.
- **No goal, real learning** — the missing deliverable is the point.
- **Surprise is the payload** — chase what breaks and surprises.
- **Play before you depend** — rehearse unfamiliar tools before trusting them in production.
- **Idle time is development time** — a drowsy loop should play, not just sleep.
- **Harvest or it's just entertainment** — end with one takeaway handed to memory.
- **Recombine** — play is where novel skill combinations get discovered.
- **Stay in the playground** — exploration never crosses a boundary.
